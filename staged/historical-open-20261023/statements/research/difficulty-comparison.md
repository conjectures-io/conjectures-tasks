# Difficulty comparison, 2026-10-21

The main comparison is with the recent Math30 research-target batch. Its
historical activation matrix is at
`output/mathlib-headers-rollout-20261018/tasks/staged/research-targets/decision-matrix.json`.
The matrix records the original activation decisions, not subsequent holds
or resolutions. In particular, its Wilf embedding-dimension-four row was
later held; that row is not used as a currently open baseline here.

Relevant previous targets include:

- Math30 4–6: restricted lonely-runner classification, four-speed spectrum,
  and a six-speed denominator bound.
- Math30 10–11: two specified families of graceful trees.
- Math30 16: complex 4-by-4 matrix multiplication with at most 47 products.
- Math30 23: Wilf's inequality under conductor at most four times multiplicity.
- Math30 25–26: the third and fourth planar Dirichlet Pólya inequalities.
- Math30 28–30: polynomial minimal-graph questions, including dimension eight.

The October 19–23 announcement packet in
`output/weekly-posts-20261019/v4/README.txt` also concerns some of these
restricted targets and Erdős 1057 and 354(i). It explicitly says that no
posting or scheduling was performed by preparation of that packet. We do not
infer actual publication from the existence of those drafts.

Assessment: **the new collection is generally more ambitious**, particularly
the full Tutte flow, Berge–Fulkerson, Lovász, and toughness conjectures.
Resolving one would settle a central longstanding universal problem. Several
old targets instead isolate a precise parameter range or finite-dimensional
threshold, giving a more focused attack surface. This is a qualitative
research judgment informed by scope and the cited literature, not a proved
ordering of mathematical difficulty. Restricted problems can still be very
hard; matrix rank and the old analytic targets prevent a defensible claim
that every new entry is harder than every old one. There is no justified
numerical success probability for either list.

The older September 21 classical-pool batch also included RH, Goldbach, twin
primes, and the inverse Galois problem, as documented in
`output/release-20261013/validator/docs/review-decisions/2026-09-21-classical-conjectures/REVIEW.md`.
Against that batch, a blanket claim that the new problems are harder would
not be justified.

The new list overshot the original preference for hard problems that might be
realistic near-term targets. Keeping full statements does not require choosing
the most famous universal conjectures. Future replacements should be narrower
original standalone questions, with historical provenance, not newly weakened
versions of the retained conjectures.


## Eight additions, 2026-10-22

The new entries are full historical questions, with no dimension, graph-order,
or special-matrix restriction added to make them easier. In particular, the
progression ratio retains every fixed length k >= 3 from the 1975 paper.
Cantoni, critical minimum degree, degenerate quadruples and Hadamard permanent
nonvanishing provide concrete, less prominent questions. That focus does not
establish that they are easy or easier than all the earlier Math30 targets.
The unrestricted Minkowski product and Marcus–de Oliveira conjectures are
particularly ambitious. No numerical difficulty score or success forecast is
justified. The final two entries are Marcus's full block-permanent conjecture
(including equality) and Wang's complete Hadamard permanent question; the
provisional random-walk and infinite-cardinal problems are not in the registry.
